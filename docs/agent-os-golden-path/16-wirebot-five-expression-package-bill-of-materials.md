# Wirebot five-expression package bill of materials

**Package-family version:** 0.1.0  
**Date:** November 19, 2026  
**Models:** Proto → Tempo → Crew → Chief → Sovereign  
**Status:** Detailed installer contract; implementation and commercial entitlement proof remain required.

## 1. Invariants for every expression

Every expression receives identity, owner/tenant binding, authority boundaries, evidence discipline, Operator World Model compatibility, conceptual-spatial reasoning, Focusa and UIAI connection checks, correction handling, privacy controls, recovery metadata, and one verified first-value task.

Every expression remains bounded by its licensed Wirebot package. Independent customer-owned Pi agents, Focusa rights, UIAI rights, schedulers, cron jobs, models, data, and machines are detected and preserved. The installer cannot disable or confiscate them because a Wirebot model is lower, expires, or changes.

## 3. Proto package — Maker

**Purpose:** Complete bounded useful tasks with a safe, evidence-backed Wirebot expression.

**Installed:** baseline identity and authority; Pi/Focusa/UIAI integration or compatible approved adapters; current-task world-model packet; approved core skill pack; one channel adapter; evidence, correction, effect verification, health and recovery checks.

**Not installed by Wirebot:** recurring schedules, recurring routines, Wirebot alarms, event watchers, workforce composer, Foreman, packaged specialist workers, Chief-of-Staff pulse, owner-wide monitoring, or a dedicated SOVOS environment.

Proto may answer, research, create, and execute an authorized bounded task during an active interaction. It does not continue by itself after the task ends. Customer-owned cron, Pi subagents, or independently licensed Focusa features remain untouched but are not represented as Proto package features.

**First-value test:** one bounded task reaches its intended destination and the installer verifies the effect.

## 4. Tempo package — Steward

**Delta from Proto:** repeatable routines, scheduling, triggers, alarms, follow-through, and routine receipts through the actual owning scheduler.

**Installed:** a selected routine catalog; schedule and trigger definitions; alarm severity and routing; deduplication; quiet hours; retry/backoff; missed-run recovery; pause/resume; owner-visible routine state; and routine-specific world-model updates.

**Not installed:** Wirebot Workforce Composer, Foreman, specialist worker pool, multi-agent dispatch, or persistent owner-wide Chief coordination. A Tempo routine can call a bounded tool or single task agent only when that right exists independently; it cannot present that as a Crew workforce.

**Alarm rule:** every alarm names its source, threshold, severity, owner, destination, suppression window, acknowledgment, recovery action, and closure evidence. No generic alarm feed.

**First-value test:** one routine runs naturally on schedule, produces the intended receiver effect, and exercises one safe failure/recovery path.

## 5. Crew package — Foreman

**Delta from Tempo:** packaged workforces and bounded multi-agent execution.

**Installed:** Workforce Composer; approved workforce templates; Foreman/supervisor role; specialist roster; dispatch permissions; concurrency and budget limits; task isolation; handoff contracts; worker evidence; parent acceptance; stop/resume/replacement; and workforce-aware alarms.

**Not installed:** persistent owner-wide Chief-of-Staff authority, cross-life/business portfolio prioritization, or a universal operating cadence. Each workforce stays inside a named mission, data boundary, authority grant, and completion standard.

A workforce is active only when native worker records and recent meaningful actions exist. Reports, scripts, schedules, cached status files, and finished sessions are not active workers.

**First-value test:** a Foreman dispatches a bounded workforce, each specialist produces inspectable evidence, the Foreman integrates the result, an independent acceptance check passes, and the intended destination receives it.

## 6. Chief package — Chief of Staff

**Delta from Crew:** persistent owner-wide life and business coordination.

**Installed:** durable Chief identity and continuity; owner-wide Operator World Model; current-context and calendar/mail/CRM/finance/project connectors as authorized; Chief operating pulse; cross-domain alarms; commitment and customer follow-through; portfolio prioritization; workforce selection and supervision; material-risk escalation; owner conversation routing; and accessible delivery.

Chief decides which routine or workforce should act, reconciles cross-system effects, and keeps initiative tied to the owner’s current world. Chief does not gain signature, recovery-code, legal-identity, hidden surveillance, or unregistered authority.

Chief can run on a qualified managed isolated runtime. A dedicated customer VPS or tailnet is not implied by the Chief model.

**First-value test:** the Chief detects or receives a real cross-domain obligation, selects the correct routine/workforce, completes and verifies the effect, updates the owner-wide model, and communicates only the useful result.

## 7. Sovereign package — Operating Partner

**Delta from Chief:** complete SOVOS deployment and owner-controlled operating environment as defined by the accepted agreement.

**Installed:** dedicated persistent owner environment; private mesh; owner-controlled local body where contracted; durable Operating Partner identity; portable memory and data custody; full portfolio and workforce governance; custom domain/routine/workforce packages; private provider and model policy where allowed; backup, migration, failover, export, and owner-control operations; comprehensive observability and recovery.

Sovereign remains bounded by law, signed terms, registered authority, privacy, safety, third-party access, and owner-reserved actions. “Sovereign” does not mean unbounded autonomy.

**First-value test:** prove owner control, dedicated topology, private connectivity, backup/restore, authority enforcement, full Chief workflow, workforce operation, data export, failure recovery, and one accepted real-world outcome.

## 8. Installer manifest fields

Every model manifest must declare:

- package and schema version;
- compatible Core, Focusa, UIAI, SOVOS, and Operator World Model versions;
- required, optional, forbidden, independently-owned, and unresolved components;
- automation, alarm, workforce, Chief, and topology policy;
- skill, role, domain, and channel packs;
- license issuer and protected admission points;
- supported platforms and hosting profiles;
- install/reuse/repair/leave-alone decisions;
- migrations, rollback, cancellation, data export, and support ownership;
- acceptance scenarios and receiver evidence.

Unknown model or entitlement data fails closed. The installer never silently selects a higher model.

## 9. Install and upgrade flow

1. Verify purchase, issuer, owner, model, and independent product rights.
2. Discover Greenfield, Brownfield, or mixed target state.
3. Calculate component decisions: reuse, install, repair, leave alone, unknown.
4. Present the exact delta and impactful changes.
5. Apply through each component’s owning installer.
6. Verify component health and protected execution admission.
7. Run the model-specific first-value and failure-recovery tests.
8. Write nonsecret receipts and owner-accessible release notes.
9. Support safe reentry, upgrade, downgrade, suspension, restoration, export, and rollback.

An upgrade applies only the next model’s delta. A downgrade removes Wirebot-packaged rights without destroying customer data or independently owned capabilities.

## 10. Remaining decisions before public release

Exact commercial SKUs, pricing, usage limits, support terms, license mappings, operating costs, supported platforms, managed-hosting topology, and independently licensed product combinations still require their owning approvals and end-to-end proof. These unresolved commercial fields do not change the behavioral separation above.
